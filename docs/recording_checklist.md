# Recording checklist

Use original video for action evidence. Ordered photos are useful inventory/reference material, but do not replace the timestamped video required by this project. A single uninterrupted whole-job recording is not mandatory: separate original clips can be used if order and recording gaps remain explicit.

Before recording, provide one photo of an available candidate kit for suitability review. A small household non-powered building-block model is a candidate only if it truly contains 10-15 individually tracked pieces. Count fasteners separately; do not pad the count with unrelated objects. No purchase or unsafe disassembly is needed.

Required:
1. Use the reviewed benign kit. Avoid electrical/battery repair, sharp parts, pressure or stored springs. Stop if a part sticks or needs force.
2. Document the complete starting assembly and all 10-15 inventory pieces with stable labels. Show a labeled tray and account for every piece. Photos can support this inventory.
3. Keep the relevant part, hands/work surface and destination visible. Perform one clear action at a time and let the before/after state be seen. Do not claim hidden actions occurred.
4. Preserve original video files and their sequence/timebase. Retain originals if making edited copies. Record gaps and off-camera movements explicitly; do not stitch away uncertainty.
5. Capture only consenting people and the work surface. Exclude faces, private documents/screens and bystanders. Audio is off by default.

The first evidence gate needs one short original clip with at least one visible, manually labelable action and identifiable part/location. Final acceptance needs evidence covering the chosen kit's reviewed workflow and a separately reviewed reassembly procedure, plus the planned interruption/comparison evaluation. Reverse disassembly footage is not an established assembly procedure. There is no arbitrary minimum number of actions beyond covering that actual procedure and every tracked piece.

Recommended: stable overhead/angled framing, even lighting, clear background, labels to distinguish similar parts, and brief pauses before/after each placement. No fixed video duration, resolution or frame rate is required. The original brief's 720p suggestion is a proposed processing budget, not an acceptance threshold. Keep recordings private; a portfolio video needs separate consent/review.
